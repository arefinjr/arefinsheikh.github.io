Titan V3 — Large-Format 3D Printer

Co-designed the mechanical structure of a commercial 3D printer with a 500 × 500 × 400 mm build volume — achieving sub-millimeter accuracy at scale.

04

Overview

Precision at industrial scale

I co-designed the mechanical structure of the Titan V3, a commercial large-format FFF 3D printer with a 500 × 500 × 400 mm build volume. At this scale, frame rigidity and motion system quality are the primary determinants of print accuracy — any deflection, vibration, or resonance translates directly into visible print artifacts.

I executed comprehensive motion system simulations and frame rigidity analysis to map natural frequencies, eliminate resonance-prone speed ranges, and minimize deflection under dynamic toolhead loads. The goal was sub-millimeter positional accuracy across the full build volume — including the corners where accumulated errors are most pronounced.

The frame design balances structural stiffness with thermal stability, accessibility for maintenance, and cost-effective manufacturing. Material and orientation were optimized for structural prints that serve as both prototyping and end-use parts in demanding applications.

Engineering Challenges

Technical Approach

Motion System Simulation

Dynamic simulation of the gantry and toolhead motion system — mapping acceleration profiles, jerk limits, and belt tensions to achieve smooth, artifact-free printing at production speeds.

Resonance Elimination

Modal analysis identified natural frequency modes that could be excited during printing. Frame geometry and bracing were tuned to push resonant frequencies well above the operational speed envelope.

Frame Rigidity Engineering

Structural frame design optimized for minimal deflection under dynamic toolhead loads — ensuring consistent first-layer adhesion and dimensional accuracy across the full 500 mm build plate.
